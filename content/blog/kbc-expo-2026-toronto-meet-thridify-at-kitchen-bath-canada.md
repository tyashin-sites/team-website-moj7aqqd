## Why kitchen and bath is a 3D problem

Most kitchen and bath products are sold as combinations. A vanity is a width, a carcass finish, a top, a sink, a faucet and a hardware choice. A kitchen run is all of that, times ten, plus a countertop edge profile. The catalogue photo shows one combination. The customer is buying a different one.

That gap shows up in places every showroom and dealer will recognise:

- The customer can't picture their finish, so they ask for samples, then a second visit, then a drawing.
- Sales reps spend days turning a rough brief into a quote, and the quote is often the first time the customer sees a price.
- Dealers carry a fraction of the range on the floor, so most SKUs are sold from a binder or a PDF.
- Size is guessed. A 36-inch vanity looks very different in a powder room than it did in the catalogue.

None of these are solved by better photography. They're solved by letting the customer build the thing and see it, at true scale, before anyone writes up an order.

## What I want to talk about at KBC Expo

The meeting is 30 minutes and we'll shape it around your products. These are the three conversations I expect to have most.

### 1. A 3D configurator for cabinets, vanities and countertops

A [3D product configurator](/3d-product-configurator) lets a customer pick door style, finish, width, top and hardware, and see the result update live in 3D. For made-to-order kitchen and bath brands, the configuration can carry straight through to a price, a quote request and a bill of materials, so the sales team starts from a spec instead of a phone call.

If you sell modular kitchens or wardrobes, our [modular kitchens page](/industries/modular-kitchens) walks through how this works for that category. We've also written about [reducing buyer confusion in modular kitchens with 3D configurators](/blog/reducing-buyer-confusion-in-modular-kitchens-with-3d-configurators).

### 2. AR for fixtures, faucets and sanitaryware

With an [AR viewer](/ar-viewer), a shopper points their phone at the wall and sees the vanity, toilet or freestanding tub in their own bathroom, at real size. It runs in the phone's browser, so there's nothing to install. For luxury sanitaryware, where the finish and proportion are the whole sale, that's the closest thing to a showroom visit you can put on a product page. More on the category on our [sanitaryware and bath page](/industries/sanitaryware).

For full rooms rather than single products, the [Room Visualizer](/room-visualizer) lets a brand stage a complete kitchen or bathroom that the shopper can then restyle.

### 3. Quoting and lead follow-up

A lot of the time in a kitchen or bath sale goes into the back-and-forth before a customer sees a number. I'm happy to talk through where configurator data and AI can shorten that: quote requests that arrive already specified, faster first responses to leads, and follow-ups that don't slip when the team is busy with installs.
