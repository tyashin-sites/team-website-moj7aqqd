Every furniture brand already has a Shop the Look. It is the lifestyle photograph on the homepage: a room the team spent two days styling, lit and shot so that every piece in it looks like it belongs. Then the page underneath lists the sofa, and the rug, the lamp and the side table are left for the shopper to go and find.

A Shop the Look page in 3D is the same room, styled by the same team, that a shopper can open, change and buy from. This post is about how that works in practice, who in the company should own it, and the three ways teams get it wrong.

## The mechanics: publish from the admin, open on the storefront

In Thridify, curated looks are rooms. Your merchandiser opens the Room Visualizer in the admin, picks a room style, places products from the published catalogue in the configurations they want to show, dresses the floor and walls, hangs the art, places the lights, and saves the room with a name.

A saved room is private until it is published. Flipping the **Published** switch makes it appear in the Rooms tab of the brand's hosted [Shop the Look page](https://thridify.com/shop-the-look), under *Curated looks*. Shoppers open it and it becomes their own draft: every product is still live, still configurable, still removable. The brand's look is the starting point, not a locked image.

That round trip is the whole feature. Everything else is detail.

## What a good curated look contains

Styling for 3D is slightly different from styling for a photograph, because the shopper will move the camera.

**Three to seven products.** Enough to make a room, few enough that each one is a decision the shopper can take or leave. A curated look with twenty items is a showroom, and showrooms are for browsing, not buying.

**Products in the configurations you want to sell.** The configuration is stored on each placed item, so the sofa in the look is the sofa in *that* fabric. If the terracotta colourway is the one with margin, style the look in terracotta.

**A room that gets out of the way.** The six built-in room styles exist for a reason: a Bright Showroom is neutral and even, a Cozy Bedroom is warm and low. Pick the one that flatters the material. Walls fade as the camera orbits so they never block the view.

**One rug, one light, one piece of art.** These are the items shoppers forget to buy and the ones with the highest attach rate when they are in the room. The rug library, the wall art and the pendant lights are there to make the look complete, and to put those products in the basket.

**A saved viewpoint.** The camera position you save with the room is the first thing the shopper sees. Set it the way a photographer would set the hero shot.

## Who should own it

Not the 3D team, and not engineering. Curated looks are merchandising. The person who styles the showroom and signs off the catalogue photography is the person who should build the looks, and the tool is built so that they can, in a browser, without a model being touched.

The practical cadence that works: one look per hero collection at launch, refreshed when the collection's lead colourway changes. A seasonal look (the festive living room, the summer terrace) on the same schedule as the campaign photography. Retire looks when the products in them are discontinued; the room will warn about a product that can no longer load, but a look with a gap in it is a bad first impression.

## Three ways teams get it wrong

**Styling a room nobody can afford.** The look with the twelve-seat dining table and the chandelier photographs beautifully and sells nothing, because the shopper opening it is furnishing a two-bedroom flat. Style for the room your median customer actually has. The room dimensions are editable for exactly this reason.

**Leaving the room empty.** A Shop the Look page with no published looks drops the shopper into an empty studio with a catalogue to one side. Some will build a room from scratch. Most will leave. Publish at least two looks before you share the link anywhere.

**Treating it as a brochure.** The look is not finished when it is published. Watch which looks are opened, which products get swapped out, which configurations the shoppers choose instead of yours. That is merchandising data you cannot get from a photograph, and the next look should be styled from it.

## Where the page lives

Shop the Look is a hosted page on the brand's account, themed from its Brand Settings: logo, accent colour, currency, and whether prices are shown. It is switched on per account. Shoppers reach it from a link: a homepage banner, a QR on a showroom wall, a line in the order confirmation email, a tablet on an exhibition stand.

The exhibition case deserves its own mention. A booth tablet running the brand's Shop the Look lets a salesperson open the look that matches the visitor's taste, swap the fabric to the one they liked, and hand the whole room to their phone through the AR QR code. The visitor leaves with their room, in your products, in their camera roll. The printed catalogue cannot do that.

## What it is not, yet

A shopper cannot save and share their own version of a look from the hosted page today; that, along with a bill of materials for the placed products and an embeddable version for product pages, is on the roadmap. The curated looks, the configurator inside the room, the material and rug libraries, the plan and walk views and the whole-room AR are live.
